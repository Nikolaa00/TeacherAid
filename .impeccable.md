## Design Context

### Users
Two screens, two people, one minute.
- **Host screen** — the presenter (later: a teacher) standing at a projector in a lit room,
  glancing at it from 2–4 m while talking. Needs: instant legibility at distance, a
  counter they can read without squinting, nothing that needs a mouse. Keyboard only.
- **Phone screen** — judges at Startup Weekend (later: 16-year-olds in a classroom) holding
  their own phone, one-handed, for the first time, with no instructions and about ten
  seconds of patience. Needs: one obvious tap per screen, big targets, no typing unless
  they want to.

### Brand Personality
Quick, warm, unbothered. A good teacher: relaxed authority with a sense of humor. The
product's line is "every class starts with a question" — the tone is curious, never
loud. Not Kahoot's carnival, not Google's blandness, not a kids' app.

### Aesthetic Direction
Physical object: a school notebook cover and the chalkboard behind it. Three materials,
inverted between the two screens:
- **Paper** — warm off-white, the host surface (a projector in a lit room needs a light
  ground; dark screens wash out).
- **Ink** — warm near-black, the type and the bars.
- **Amber** — the bell's brass; the accent on the host, the *surface* on the phone. The
  phone is a game controller: a full amber screen with ink type and ink buttons; the
  result screen flips to ink with amber type.
Typography: **Bitter** (slab serif, variable, Cyrillic) for questions, numbers and
nicknames — slab numerals read from the back row and say "textbook", not "quiz app";
**Sofia Sans** (variable, Cyrillic, Balkan-made) for UI text. Both carry Cyrillic so the
same system survives the switch to Macedonian.
Anti-references: purple/indigo quiz apps, claymorphism, rounded-everything, confetti
cannons, gradients, dark mode with glow, emoji as icons.
Theme: light on the host, amber on the phone, ink for the phone's result state. No dark
mode toggle.

### Design Principles
1. **One thing per screen.** Each phase shows exactly what the person needs to do or
   read now. Progressive disclosure across phases, not within a screen.
2. **Legible at 4 metres.** Host type starts at 32 px; the question and the counter are
   the biggest things in the room.
3. **One tap, big target.** Every phone action is a ≥ 56 px button; the reason box is
   optional and appears only after a choice.
4. **Motion is the reveal.** One orchestrated moment (bars settle, points count, rows
   stagger). Everything else is a 200 ms state change. Reduced motion respected.
5. **Nothing to explain.** If a screen needs a sentence of instructions, the screen is
   wrong.
